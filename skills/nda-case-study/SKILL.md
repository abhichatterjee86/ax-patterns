# A case study you are allowed to show

Most of a working designer's best work is under NDA. The usual answers - blurred screenshots, a password, or nothing - all lose. This skill keeps the real problem, the real decisions and the real lessons, and changes everything that identifies the client.

## Procedure

1. **Ask what may be shown.** Before writing anything, ask the designer what their contract or NDA allows: the client's name, the industry, the problem, the timeline, any screens. If they are unsure, assume nothing may be shown and say so. This skill is not legal advice.
2. **Build a fictional world, once.**
   - A fictional company in the same industry, with a plain name. Keep the industry: it is what makes the problem believable.
   - If the industry plus the details would still point to 1 client (a small market, a famous product), generalise further until it would not.
   - Replace every client name, logo, colour, person, project name, product name, ID format and data value.
   - A small cast of 4 to 6 people with full names and roles, reused across every case study, so the portfolio reads as 1 world.
   - Emails on the reserved `.example` domain. Never a real domain.
   - A disclaimer line on every page: "A portfolio concept by <name>. Every company, person and record in it is invented."
3. **Rebuild, do not screenshot.** Rebuild the key screens as a clickable prototype in your own neutral design system. Every image in the case study is a capture of that prototype, not a mockup. If a figure shows the design as it was delivered, label it ("As delivered").
4. **Tell the story in this order:**
   - **In 1 minute**: 4 or 5 short rows - the problem, the main decision, what was built, what changed after feedback, what was added after delivery.
   - **Facts**: role, timeline, context, output.
   - **Chapters**, each opening with 1 sentence that says what happened, and closing with "where this left the work".
   - **Wrong turns**, each as 3 lines: what I drew, what broke it, what replaced it.
   - **Quotes** credited to a role ("Product owner"), never a name, and only if the contract allows you to use what people said.
   - **Walk it yourself**: a link into the prototype, per persona.
5. **Write a decisions log** beside it. Each decision: an ID (d01, d02...), a short title, 1 or 2 paragraphs, the cost of the decision, and a status - delivered, prototype only, or superseded (and by what). Link claims in the story to their decision ID.

## Honesty rules

- Say clearly what was delivered to the client and what was designed afterwards for the portfolio.
- No invented numbers. If there is no measured result, say what changed in words, or leave it out.
- State your role precisely, and credit the team.
- A superseded decision stays in the log. Showing what you changed your mind about is the strongest part of a case study.

## Writing

Plain words, short sentences, numerals ("3 roles"), sentence case, no buzzwords. Every chapter title says something on its own ("Stewards needed to see why, not just what"), not a label ("Research").

## Done when

- No real client name, logo, person, colour, ID or data appears anywhere, including image file names and alt text.
- A recruiter can read the "In 1 minute" card and say what the problem was and what you decided.
- Every image is a capture of the prototype, or labelled as delivered.
- Every decision named in the story has an entry in the log.
- Nothing claims a result that was not measured.
